# Ketamine as a second-line drug for benzodiazepine-refractory seizures (evidence for a contract entry)

Owner, #103 review (2026-09-28): "If patient does not respond to benzos, you can offer levetiracetam, so Ketamine is a known second and third line drug for SZ. status SZ. Most rescue and ems systems dont have levetiracetam. There is data behind it. Search and include KetamINE for second line."

**Status (2026-09-28): option B signed by the owner in #104.** Adult IV/IO 2 mg/kg, IM 3–4 mg/kg (served as 3 mg/kg, a range's minimum); child IV/IO 1 mg/kg, IM 3 mg/kg, 3 months and over. It is owner-declared, since no approved source states a ketamine seizure dose. The ACTIVE SEIZURE card serves it as the second drug after a benzodiazepine, with levetiracetam as the alternative. The rest of this page is the evidence behind that decision.

|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **JTS Prolonged Casualty Care Guidelines, CPG ID91 (21 Dec 2021), p.28** (repeated p.29), in the corpus: "Immediate seizure treatment with benzodiazepines, consider ketamine for refractory seizures." | **The indication**, in a JTS CPG | No |
| 2 | **Scheppke KA, Pepe PE, et al. Effectiveness of Ketamine As a Rescue Drug for Patients Experiencing Benzodiazepine-Resistant Status Epilepticus in the Prehospital Setting. Crit Care Explor. 2024;6(12):e1186.** doi:10.1097/CCE.0000000000001186. PMID 39642307. A 7-year, population-based observational study, Palm Beach County Fire Rescue. | Adults: ketamine "rapidly terminated convulsions in 56 (98.2%)" of 57 after sufficient midazolam. "No identifiable clinically significant complications attributable to ketamine" in 81 cases. | **Adults and adolescents: 100 mg IV/IO/IM/IN. Children: 1 mg/kg IM/IN.** The protocol dose. |
| 3 | **Finney JD, et al. Prehospital Ketamine Administration in Benzodiazepine Refractory Status Epilepticus: A Case Series Review. Prehosp Emerg Care. 2026;30(2):323-331.** doi:10.1080/10903127.2025.2486302. PMID 40193549. A retrospective case series of 42 patients (8 months to 79 years), one urban EMS system, 2021-2023. | "Ketamine stopped seizures in 38 patients (90.5%)." Transient hypoxia in 9 (22%), BVM in 13 (31%), SGA in 3, one intubation. The authors say these "rates … are consistent with expected rates from seizures and appropriate benzodiazepine dosing". | **Doses given, not a protocol:** IM mean 3.3 mg/kg; IV/IO mean 2.2 mg/kg. |
| 4 | **Othman AA, et al. Seizures stopped at 5 min in 76% against 21%, and intubation was 4.2% against 20.8%. First-line add-on, not second line. | The abstract doesn't state the dose. |

## Dose options for the owner to rule on

The evidence for a *prehospital second-line* dose is two observational studies (sources 2 and 3). No randomized trial of second-line ketamine was found.

| Option | Adult | Child | Basis | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **100 mg** IV/IO/IM/IN, fixed | 1 mg/kg IM/IN | Source 2's protocol, the only protocol dose with published outcomes (56/57 adults) | A fixed adult dose needs no weight, which matters in a seizing patient. |
| **B** | 2 mg/kg IV/IO; 3–4 mg/kg IM | as the signed paediatric dissociative doses | Source 3's observed means (IV 2.2, IM 3.3 mg/kg) | These are the doses already signed for adult dissociative sedation (NASEMSO: 2 mg/kg IV, 4 mg/kg IM). Needs a weight. |

For either option:
- **Indication:** "refractory seizure (benzodiazepine-refractory)". The card's lookup already matches it.
- **Sources:** JTS ID91 p.28 for the indication, plus source 2 or 3 for the dose.
- **Cautions:** from source 3: hypoxia 22%, BVM 31%, so be ready to ventilate.
- **Contraindications:** as ketamine's other entries.
