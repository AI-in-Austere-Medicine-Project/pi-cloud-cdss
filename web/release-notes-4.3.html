Overview

Version 4.1 was a hardening release found by auditing field logs. Version 4.2 built out patient context — vitals, derived MAP, temperature — and put the system on a multi-provider model grid. Version 4.3 answers a different question: what does EdgeCDSS do when the guideline corpus does not contain the answer?

Until now there were two possible sources for a response: a Joint Trauma System guideline passage, or a general medical reference fallback labelled as such. Both are retrieval over text somebody else wrote. Neither is a clinician saying this is the setting, for this physiology, and I stand behind it. Version 4.3 adds that third source — a clinical card — and builds a gate around it that makes an unsigned card indistinguishable from an absent one.

Retrieved → Generated → Authored

A card is not a better prompt and it is not a bigger corpus. It is a small, dated, referenced clinical statement with a named signing role attached, served verbatim. The engineering that matters is not the card format — it is the fence that refuses to serve one nobody has signed.

What's new in 4.3

A DKA ventilator question now returns ventilator settings

  • The finding. The round-1 evaluation measured 0 of 4 DKA vent phrasings returning any of VT, RR, PEEP or FiO2, against 4 of 4 for traumatic brain injury — 100% reproducible. TBI was answered well by retrieval. DKA fell through to prose that never reached a number.
  • DKA is now 4 of 4. All four phrasings reach the metabolic_acidosis card and return all four settings. TBI holds at 4 of 4 — the control that proves the module did not buy one physiology at another's expense.
  • The card says the thing the corpus never did: match the compensatory minute ventilation, do not set a normal rate. A patient breathing 35–40 is defending their pH, and 12–16 halves their minute ventilation.

One SBP target, not three

  • Three TBI answers gave three different systolic targets — the same question, asked three ways, returning three numbers. The tbi card carries one: SBP ≥ 110 mmHg.
  • A card is where a number stops depending on which answer you happened to get.

The authorship fence

This is the part of 4.3 worth reading twice. Everything else in the release is content; this is the control that makes the content trustworthy.

  • The engine refuses to serve a card whose clinical fields are still PENDING_CLINICAL_SIGNOFF, whose signoff flag is not true, whose reviewer is not an authorised signature, or whose references are empty. A half-authored card is indistinguishable from an absent one: the query falls through to whatever answered it before the module existed.
  • There is no override. The project's debug flag downgrades safety holds elsewhere in the system and does not reach this gate. A test asserts the flag's name does not appear in the module at all, and that the servability check takes no parameter a bypass could ride in on.
  • Cards go live one at a time. Partial deployment is the normal state, not a migration step. Five physiology cards are live; four troubleshooting cards and four device cards are authored, unsigned, dark, and invisible to the pipeline.
  • Signing authorises the signature and nothing more. An authorised signature on a card still holding a placeholder is refused for the content, not for the name.
  • A signed card must carry no placeholder in any field, including the ones outside the gated clinical set. One render path printed a caveat field verbatim, so a truthy placeholder would have put the literal string PENDING_CLINICAL_SIGNOFF in front of a medic. Guarded, and a test now checks every field rather than the gated ones.

Tidal volume is dosed on ideal body weight

  • Height and sex are now captured, and tidal volume is dosed on Devine ideal body weight rather than actual weight. A 75 kg casualty at 178 cm is 439 mL at 6 mL/kg, not 450.
  • Missing height does not guess. VT falls back to actual weight, says so on the settings line — 450 mL (ACTUAL weight 75.0 kg — not IBW) — and the card adds its own caveat naming that VT a ceiling until a height is entered.
  • An ambiguous bare measurement is rejected rather than guessed, a height cannot be swallowed as a blood pressure, and a patient boundary clears it like every other vital.

Provenance: a third source mode

  • VENT_CARD joins the JTS corpus and general reference as a distinct provenance value — logged as itself, never as a synonym for JTS. The served line reads “reviewed by clinician, <date>” alongside the card's own references.
  • Cards are signed by role rather than by name. A role identifies nobody, so the line tells a medic that a clinician stands behind the card and when they signed it, but not which clinician. Deployments that need an auditable signer can configure real names.

Dispatch: the baseline card is no longer the silent default

  • The lung-protective baseline card also matched “vent settings” and “set the vent”, which made it the first match for nearly every real ventilator question and shadowed all four specific cards — a DKA query reached the ARDS card, which is the original defect with the roles reversed.
  • A settings question naming no physiology now asks which one, and the menu lists only cards that are actually live.
  • Troubleshooting outranks settings when alarm language is present; device aliases require ventilator context before they name a device; no ventilator pattern matches inside a longer word.

What shipped in 4.2

Version 4.2 never got its own notes page. It was a large release and the short version is here; the long version is in the changelog.

Patient context: vitals, derived MAP, temperature

  • Vitals are parsed from free text and held in session context — HR, BP, SpO2, RR, GCS and temperature, in the phrasings a medic actually types. Every reading carries the timestamp of the turn it was stated in, and a turn with no timestamp yields a reading whose age is unknown, never a fabricated “just now”.
  • MAP is derived whenever a pressure is recorded, shown beside it, and labelled derived everywhere it appears — because it is the first value in the vitals block the system produces rather than hears. It catches the pressure a systolic threshold does not: BP 90/30 has an SBP that is not below 90 and a MAP of 50. A stated MAP off an arterial line supersedes the derived one; arithmetic does not overrule a measurement.
  • Impossible values are rejected visibly, not silently, and a vitals conflict produces a caution that downgrades a SAFE verdict to human review — it can never block a response and, the direction that would actually be dangerous, it can never release one.
  • Vitals never compute a dose. Pinned by a test that builds the dose contract with and without vitals present and asserts it is identical.

The validator now hears a statement, not a silence

  • The patient block states the age band on every response — pediatric, adult, not-pediatric, or unknown. It used to assert the status only when true and say nothing when false, so “known adult” and “nobody has said” were the same silence. A validator reviewing a 77.1 kg casualty said so out loud: it had the weight, it did not have the band.
  • Unknown is not adult. With no age and no weight the block says UNKNOWN rather than claiming an adult, which would be the same failure reversed.

“Milligrams” routed a casualty as being in shock

  • The shock detector matched substrings: ams inside milligrams, altered inside unaltered — an explicit negation read as its opposite. Any dose stated in grams routed as shock, and with an infection present that was the sepsis router firing on the word “milligrams”.
  • Short tokens are now word-anchored. This is the fourth specimen of the same failure class in this codebase, after the alias table, the preparation terms and the vitals labels — which is why it is written down as a class rather than as four bugs.

Round-1 evaluation: 160 scenarios, measured before and after

A standing evaluation harness now replays 160 scenarios against a pinned server snapshot — 62 real queries extracted from session logs, the v4.1 audit's safety cases replayed with their turn sequences, a sample of the gate-log invariant matrix, and 75 authored scenarios. Same corpus, same model, same held validator; only the server snapshot differs.

Provider grid and a security patch

  • Models are configuration, not code. OpenAI, Anthropic, Google Gemini, xAI and self-hosted local inference are all reachable from one registry file; adding a model is an edit, not a change. Model identifiers are verified against the live APIs rather than taken from memory, and the file records which ones round-tripped and which are gated by account state.
  • Four exploitable findings were closed in an emergency patch — an unauthenticated write endpoint, a summary endpoint that returned raw log lines including submitter IPs and free-text clinical queries, a synchronous pipeline blocking the event loop that answers the health check a watchdog reads, and a secret-redactor that matched two key shapes and missed three providers. Redaction is now driven by the provider registry's own declarations, so a provider added there is redacted the moment it is declared.
  • The audit that found them was also corrected where it was wrong: one claimed log-forgery vector did not exist, was verified not to exist, and is withdrawn in the audit document rather than quietly dropped.

How this was found

Version 4.1's lesson was audit your logs, not your test results. Version 4.3's is the next one along: build the harness that turns an anecdote into a number. “The vent answers feel thin” is an opinion nobody can act on. “0 of 4 DKA phrasings return any of four settings, 100% reproducible, against 4 of 4 for TBI” is a defect with a name, a control group, and a definition of done.

The working rule from 4.1 still holds — one fix, one commit, one regression test. The offline suite is now 859 tests, running in about eight seconds with no network, no API key, and no vector database.

Known boundaries

Eight of the thirteen ventilator cards are dark. The four troubleshooting cards and four device cards are written but unsigned, and the engine treats them as absent — a troubleshooting question routes to whatever answered it before the module existed. That is the intended behaviour of the fence, and it is also a real gap in coverage until a clinician signs them. Partial deployment is the normal state here, not a migration step.
Two of the eight configured models are selectable but not usable for sustained traffic. Availability is gated per provider, not per model. The Gemini free tier authenticates and then caps at 20 requests per day per model, so a medic can pick a model that fails once the cap is hit — fail-closed, and still a dead menu entry. Documented in the registry file rather than hidden.
EdgeCDSS is decision support, not a decision-maker. It is a research prototype evaluated with simulated and synthetic scenarios only — not validated for clinical use and not for patient care decisions. Language generation currently requires connectivity to a hosted model; the generation layer is deliberately swappable and a fully offline on-device model remains the research goal (Layer 02).

The lesson

Retrieval can only return what somebody already wrote down. When the corpus is silent, a language model will still produce fluent prose — and prose that never reaches a number is the most dangerous kind of answer, because it looks like help. The fix was not a better prompt. It was a clinician writing the answer down, dating it, and a gate that refuses to serve it until they have.
